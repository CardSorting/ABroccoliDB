# Defensive Patent and IP Policy

**Project:** `BroccoliDB` / `@noorm/broccolidb`
**Project steward:** William Andrew Cruz (`CardSorting` / `bozoegg`)
**Effective release line:** `3.0.0` and later, unless a release says otherwise
**Governing software license:** Apache License, Version 2.0

This policy is the defensive-IP layer accompanying the LUMI-NEW licensing
strategy. It is a project policy and attribution record; it does not replace,
modify, or add restrictions to the Apache License. The `LICENSE` file controls
the permissions for the software. It is not a separate promise by the steward
to waive, license, or refrain from enforcing any patent right; Apache Section 3
is the only operative patent-termination term in this distribution.

## 1. Defensive patent position

The Apache License, Version 2.0 provides the operative patent grant and the
defensive patent-termination condition. In particular, a recipient’s patent
license for the Work terminates under Section 3 when that recipient institutes
patent litigation alleging that the Work or an incorporated Contribution
infringes a patent.

The project does not promise that the Work is free of third-party patent
claims, that any patent is invalid, or that this policy creates a patent
license beyond the license in `LICENSE`. No contributor should treat this
document as a freedom-to-operate opinion.

## 2. Copyright, attribution, and brand enforcement

Copyright, license, patent, and attribution notices must remain intact when
the Apache License requires them to remain intact. The project will preserve
release archives, source history, package metadata, and NOTICE content as
evidence of provenance and to make enforcement of those conditions practical.

The Apache License does not grant permission to use project names, logos, or
other marks in a way that implies endorsement or creates confusion. BroccoliDB
marks are addressed separately in [TRADEMARKS.md](TRADEMARKS.md).

## 3. Defensive disclosure discipline

The [IP record](docs/ip/README.md) describes technical mechanisms and the
evidence that can be checked in this repository. A repository timestamp is not
automatically a legally effective public disclosure in every jurisdiction.
Only use a date as a prior-art date after confirming that the corresponding
commit, release, or archive was actually made publicly available and preserved.

The project will not describe an engineering note as “irrefutable,” “legally
binding prior art,” or a guarantee of patent invalidation. Those conclusions
require jurisdiction-specific legal analysis and, where appropriate, patent
counsel.

## 4. Defensive response playbook

If a patent or copyright dispute arises, preserve the exact release artifact,
its SHA-256 digest, the corresponding Git commit, the package metadata, and
the relevant NOTICE and IP records. Do not rewrite history or make public
technical admissions before obtaining qualified legal advice. The project may
provide factual provenance records and source history to counsel, courts, or
patent offices; it does not promise a particular litigation or review outcome.

## 5. Historical license boundary

BroccoliDB `2.0.x` was published under the MIT License. Existing permissions
granted for those versions are not retroactively revoked by this policy or by
the Apache-licensed `3.0.0` release line. See [the legal strategy](docs/LEGAL-STRATEGY.md)
for the migration boundary and the limits of relicensing a published release.
